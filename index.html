Experimental controls

The motion confirmation gate reduces false starts but will miss some brief or gentle movements. Compass correction defaults OFF pending validation of the device's heading convention.

These thresholds are heuristics, not physically guaranteed movement classification. Tune while stationary, then compare known movements. Disabling the compass does not disable the browser's own fused orientation.

Algorithms and limitations

  1. Sensor/frame validation. Require finite gravity-removed DeviceMotionEvent.acceleration, gyroscope rotation rates and recent orientation. Never subtract gravity twice. Transform device acceleration using browser orientation R = Rz(α)Rx(β)Ry(γ) and the calibration reference R₀ᵀR. Reject stale orientation and gaps greater than 0.25 s. The browser's fused orientation is used; this app does not estimate attitude from raw gyro integration.
  2. Stationary calibration. Collect at least 50 samples over at least 3 s with low angular speed and low acceleration variability. Estimate initial acceleration bias from the mean transformed acceleration and initial gyro offset from the mean rotation rate. Motion restarts calibration. The resulting gyro bias is used for stationary detection, not to correct the browser's internal attitude solution.
  3. Motion gate with confirmation. Evaluate a 0.4 s rolling RMS window. Motion must persist for a configurable confirmation period, unless it exceeds 1.8× the thresholds. Strong stationary hold suppresses integration before motion is confirmed. Confirmed rest requires 0.35 s of quiet samples. Constant-speed travel can still be mistaken for rest.
  4. ZUPT and ZARU. While confidently stationary or in provisional hold, apply a zero-velocity pseudo-measurement using a diagonal Kalman-style velocity covariance update and hold position. Update gyro bias using a slow EMA (ZARU), and update accelerometer bias only during confirmed stationary periods with small residual acceleration. Neither update recovers previously accumulated position error.
  5. Adaptive acceleration filtering. Low-pass corrected acceleration (0.12 s time constant), apply a small soft deadband, then trapezoidally integrate acceleration to velocity and velocity to position. Filtering reduces jitter but introduces lag and does not remove drift from moving bias.
  6. Compass-assisted yaw. When Safari supplies webkitCompassHeading and webkitCompassAccuracy with plausible accuracy, gradually adjust the reference-frame yaw of the orientation matrix toward the compass heading. Compass is rejected when unavailable or inaccurate. Compass helps heading, not displacement; magnetic interference can worsen results. Compass and orientation conventions vary by platform, so check the stationary rotation diagnostic.
  7. Velocity uncertainty. Propagate a simple diagonal velocity covariance using acceleration noise, then update with v=0 when stationary. This is not a full error-state Kalman filter: cross-axis covariance, orientation uncertainty, and position covariance are not modeled.
  8. Barometer. Pressure/altitude data are not available through standard iOS Safari web APIs. No barometric fusion is claimed or simulated. A native Core Motion implementation would be needed for barometric Z correction.

This is not reliable indoor positioning. It cannot observe constant velocity, prevent long-term horizontal position drift, or reconstruct the true path without external position/velocity references. The plots are relative estimates, not ground truth.

iOS troubleshooting and test protocol
  1. Use a physical iPhone with Safari on HTTPS. A downloaded file or embedded preview may not expose sensors.
  2. Tap Enable directly and approve motion/orientation access. If denied, review Safari website permissions or iOS settings and reload.
  3. Keep phone completely still during calibration. Avoid vibration and moving the phone while calibrating.
  4. After calibration, leave phone still for 10 seconds: velocity should remain near zero, position approximately constant and ZUPT should engage.
  5. Rotate the phone in place without translation: large displacement indicates orientation/frame or gravity-compensation problems.
  6. Move approximately 0.5 m along one axis, stop, then return. Compare residual error; export CSV for debugging.
  7. If compass reads unavailable, that is expected on some browsers; core navigation still works.
  8. If sensor samples stop in the background, return to Safari and recalibrate.
